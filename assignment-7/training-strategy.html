Training a model with RKE at the input and the head

A phased plan for training a transformer whose input embedding and output head are both the reversible byte codec: what to freeze, what to learn, how to initialise it, what to log, and the four gates at which the whole idea should be abandoned if it fails.

§1   Scope

What is being trained, and what is already decided

The architecture is fixed before training starts. The encoder is \(\mathbf e(b)=\operatorname{flat}(T\,Z(b))\,Q\) with \(Z(b)_{p,:}=g_{b_p}/\sqrt L\), and the head is its adjoint — the same \(G\), \(T\), \(Q\), reused rather than duplicated:

\[S \;=\; T\,\operatorname{unflat}(\mathbf h Q^{\top})\,G^{\top}\;\in\;\mathbb R^{P\times256},\qquad \text{logit}(t)=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{L_t}}\sum_{p=1}^{L_t}S\big[p,b^{(t)}_p\big]+\beta_t.\]

Two design decisions are settled and should not be relitigated during training runs, because both follow from proofs rather than from tuning:

  • \(G\) is frozen at an offline equiangular-tight-frame construction. The margin-optimal codebook is a property of \((256,s)\) alone; offline coherence minimisation captures 93% of the available headroom at \(s=128\) (μ 0.352 → 0.093 against a Welch floor of 0.063). Since \(Q\) is orthogonal it cannot alter relative angles between byte directions, so training \(G\) means letting the loss decide which bytes are similar — the co-occurrence collapse that byte-level embedding exists to avoid.
  • The head scores whole tokens and softmaxes over \(V\). It does not factorise into per-position byte distributions. There is no independence assumption anywhere in the loss.

What is trained: the transformer body, the orthogonal mixer \(Q\), the spectral weights \(w\), the logit scale \(\alpha\), and the per-token bias \(\beta_t\).

§2   Configuration

Pick \(P\) from the tokenizer, not from convention

The single configuration choice that can silently cripple the model is \(P\). The span of all token embeddings is \(\operatorname{span}\{T\mathbf e_p\}_{p\le L_{\max}}\otimes\operatorname{span}(G)\), so the induced embedding table has

\[\operatorname{rank} \;=\; \min\big(d,\; L_{\max}\cdot s\big),\qquad s = d/P.\]

Setting \(P\) larger than the longest token in the tokenizer wastes softmax capacity — the opposite of the intuition that more position slots is safer. Measured on a set capped at 20 bytes with \(s=128\): numerical rank 1764 of 4096.

Configuration procedure

  1. Measure the tokenizer's byte-length distribution. Record \(L_{\max}\), \(\bar L\), and the fraction of tokens above each candidate \(P\).
  2. Set \(P = L_{\max}\) if \(L_{\max}\le 32\). If the tail is long and thin, truncate at the 99.99th percentile and handle the remainder as an explicit overflow (below).
  3. Set \(s = d/P\); require \(s\ge 2\) (Theorem 4) and check the Welch floor \(\sqrt{(256-s)/(255s)}\) gives an acceptable margin.
  4. Build \(G\) offline, record \(\mu\).
  5. Collision audit. Truncate every vocabulary entry to \(P\) bytes and count duplicates. Any collision is two tokens the model can never distinguish.
Gate 0  ·  before any training

Encode the full vocabulary and decode it back in bf16. Require 100.000% exact and zero truncation collisions. If either fails, raise \(P\), or add a length-and-overflow slot, and repeat. Do not start a training run on a lossy codec — every downstream result would be uninterpretable.

§3   Optimiser

Parameter groups, and the one group that is actually at risk

RKE moves nearly the whole input/output pathway out of the sparse-gradient regime. A median BPE embedding row under a Zipf vocabulary receives well under one gradient contribution per step; \(G\), \(w\) and \(Q\) each receive \(10^4\)–\(10^6\). Rare-token undertraining — a real pathology of standard tables — simply does not arise for the geometry.

The exception is \(\beta_t\). It is one scalar per vocabulary entry, so it inherits exactly the Zipf sparsity that the rest of the design escapes. It is the group that needs care, and it is the group most likely to be given default treatment by accident.

The mistake worth pre-empting

A large effective batch means low gradient variance, which conventionally licenses a higher learning rate, not a lower one — and under Adam the magnitude is normalised away regardless. Do not reach for a reduced LR on \(G\), \(w\) or \(Q\) on the theory that they "see too much data." If instability appears there, the cause is almost certainly the manifold constraint fighting the optimiser state, not the batch.

Riemannian handling

\(Q\) lives on \(O(d)\) and \(G\) (if unfrozen) on a product of spheres. Renormalising after an Adam step while leaving momentum in the ambient space means the optimiser accumulates velocity in directions that the projection then discards, silently. Project momentum onto the tangent space before the update. This is a bug class, not a hyperparameter — it shows up as unexplained early-training instability rather than as an obvious failure.

§4   Initialisation

The head cannot learn frequency from geometry, so hand it to it

With an equal-norm codebook, \(\|\mathbf e(t)\|\) is nearly constant — measured standard deviation 2.3% of the mean across a vocabulary-like set. A free embedding table encodes token frequency in row norm; this one structurally cannot. Every bit of the unigram prior has to live in \(\beta_t\).

  • \(\beta_t \leftarrow \log \hat p(t)\) from token counts over the training corpus. This is not an optimisation nicety — without it the model opens at a uniform distribution over 131k tokens and must spend early capacity rediscovering that the is common.
  • \(\alpha\): choose so the initial logit standard deviation matches what a standard head would produce, then verify the first-step loss is close to the empirical unigram entropy rather than \(\log|V|\). A loss that starts at \(\log|V|\) and stays there is the signature of a miscalibrated \(\alpha\), not of a broken architecture.
  • \(Q\): random orthogonal for symmetry breaking. Identity is the diagnostic-friendly alternative — with \(Q=I\) the position slots are axis-aligned and every intermediate tensor is directly interpretable, which is worth having in the first debugging run.
  • \(w\): initialise to the taper giving \(\chi\approx 8\) (\(\rho(1)\approx0.68,\ \rho(2)\approx0.23\)). \(\chi=1\) reproduces the original hard-slot codec and is the control arm, not the default.

§5   Implementation

The head is a gather, not a matmul

Precompute once: a uint8 byte table of shape \((V,P)\) and a length vector \(L_t\). That is 4.2 MB at \(V=131{,}072,\ P=32\) — it replaces a 537 M-parameter output matrix.

# --- forward, per predicted position -------------------------------
H     = (h @ Q.T).view(P, s)              # undo the orthogonal mixer
S     = (T @ H) @ G.T                     # (P, 256) score matrix — the whole head
flat  = S.reshape(-1)                     # P*256 = 8192 entries
idx   = byte_table * P + arange(P)        # (V, P) precomputed, uint8 -> int32
logit = (flat[idx] * mask).sum(-1) * alpha / sqrt(L) + beta

# --- notes ---------------------------------------------------------
# T is circulant: apply as an FFT pair, O(P log P), never materialise it.
# Q is a Householder product: apply reflection-by-reflection, O(m·d).
# S in fp32. The gather-sum in fp32. CE in fp32. Master weights fp32.
# Backward through the gather is a scatter-add into S — 0.8M adds.

Two implementation traps. First, materialising \(T\) as a dense \(P\times P\) matrix and \(Q\) as a dense \(d\times d\) matrix works but throws away the structure that makes them cheap; use the FFT and the reflection sequence. Second, the gather is memory-bandwidth bound rather than compute bound, so the 455× MAC reduction will not translate into a 455× wall-clock reduction — expect the head to stop being the bottleneck rather than to become free.

§6   Phases and gates

Four decisions, in the order that kills the idea cheapest

The plan is ordered so that the experiment most likely to invalidate everything runs early and cheap. Nothing downstream is worth running if Phase 2 fails.

Phase 1  ·  Smoke test

A small model on a few thousand tokens, deliberately overfitting. The question is not quality; it is whether the tied head can express an arbitrary token distribution at all. If loss cannot be driven to near zero, the rank ceiling of §2 or the byte-similarity limit of §9 is binding, and the configuration is wrong.

Gate 1

Training loss → < 0.05 on a memorisation set. Also confirm the round-trip decoder still returns 100% exact bytes from the trained encoder.

Phase 2  ·  The decisive ablation

124 M GPT-2, three seeds, matched data, steps and compute. Four arms:

ArmInputHeadIsolates
ABPE table, tiedtiedbaseline
BKronecker, dense \(\mathbf W_{\text{proj}}\)untied standardthe published result
CRKE, \(T=I\), \(Q=I\)untied standardcost of block-orthogonality alone
DRKE fulltied to \(G\)cost of tying the head

C versus B is the load-bearing comparison and the only one that must be run first. It changes exactly one thing: a dense trained projection becomes a block-orthogonal constrained one. Everything else in the design — \(T\), \(Q\), the tied head — is an invertible addition on top of C and cannot remove information.

Gate 2  ·  the one that decides the project

Arm C validation loss within noise of arm B across three seeds (the reference gap is \(0.083\pm0.007\) nats, so "within noise" means the C–B gap is under roughly 0.02 nats). If C loses materially, the block constraint is too expensive and the correct next question is how much of it can be relaxed while keeping a closed-form inverse — not how to tune around it.

Phase 3  ·  Sweeps

Only after Gate 2. Sweep \(\chi\in\{1,2,4,8,32\}\) — it is entirely possible the transformer prefers hard position boundaries and \(\chi=1\) wins, which would be an interesting negative result about the value of the spectral kernel. Sweep Householder count \(m\in\{16,64,256\}\). Ablate \(\beta_t\) initialisation. Ablate frozen versus learned \(G\).

Gate 3

At least one \(\chi>1\) setting beats \(\chi=1\), or accept the hard-slot kernel and drop \(T\) from the design entirely — it costs 32 parameters but adds a conditioning constraint that is only worth carrying if it earns its place.

Phase 4  ·  Scale and stress

One larger run, plus the robustness properties the design is supposed to buy: spelling-perturbation stability against the paper's protocol, and out-of-vocabulary handling, since the encoder is defined on all byte strings rather than on \(V\).

Gate 4

Spelling robustness at least matches the published Kronecker numbers (top-1 preservation 55.5%, mean KL 0.79). Losing here would mean the compression to \(s=128\) has eaten the byte-level locality that motivated the approach.

§9   Risks

What could be true that would make this not work

  1. Block-orthogonality costs more than it saves. The published 2.5% advantage came from a dense unconstrained projection. Everything here constrains it. Gate 2 exists to find this out in twelve runs rather than at scale. Probability this is the outcome: material.
  2. Output-side byte similarity is binding. On the input side the body can disambiguate compute from commute downstream; at the head there is no downstream. Worst near-duplicate pair measured at cosine 0.954, leaving 31% of the embedding norm as logit-gap budget. Separable, but the head spends a third of its dynamic range on it.
  3. Tying may simply be worse at scale, as it often is for standard heads. Here untying costs 33 K parameters, so this is cheap to test and cheap to concede.
  4. Truncation collisions in a real vocabulary. Zero on random strings, but real tokenizers contain long whitespace runs and Indic word-pieces. Gate 0 catches this before any compute is spent.
  5. The gather may not be fast in practice. 455× on MACs, but memory-bandwidth bound. Budget for the head ceasing to be the bottleneck, not for it vanishing.

§10   Out of scope

Deliberately not in this plan

Open-vocabulary byte generation. Emitting arbitrary UTF-8 rather than vocabulary entries requires a factorised distribution over byte slots, and that is where the independence assumption \(P(b)=\prod_p P(b_p)\) genuinely bites — after q, u is near-certain and independent softmaxes cannot say so. It needs either vocabulary reranking or a small autoregressive-over-positions head, and it should not be entangled with the question this plan answers.

Reversibility at depth. The decoder inverts \(\mathbf e\), not a layer-12 hidden state. Information about the bytes is non-increasing through the body, and should be.

Any claim that this is established. Every quantitative statement in this document is either a proof about a linear map or a measurement of an untrained implementation. None of it is evidence about training. That is the entire purpose of Gate 2.
